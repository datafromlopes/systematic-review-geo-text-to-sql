# Systematic review: LLM-based text-to-SQL for geospatial data and Brazilian Portuguese

Artifacts of the systematic literature review reported in Chapter 3 of the master's thesis
*AtlasSQL-BR: a Brazilian Portuguese geospatial text-to-SQL dataset* (IME-USP, 2026), by
Diego Lopes. The review characterizes LLM-based text-to-SQL techniques with emphasis on
real-world and geospatial databases, on the datasets used to train and evaluate them, and on
languages other than English. Searches were run on 19 July 2026 and selection was concluded
on 31 August 2026.

Related repository: [atlas-sql-br](https://github.com/datafromlopes/atlas-sql-br), the dataset
and fine-tuning pipeline that answer the gaps identified here.

## How the review was conducted

The review followed the protocol of Kitchenham and Charters and was managed in
[Parsifal](https://parsif.al). It had three parts, carried out with different tools:

1. **Search and selection: manual.** The search string (below) was run in the ACM Digital
   Library, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, Scopus and SpringerLink, and the returned records were
   exported as BibTeX (`data/bibtex_exports/`) and imported into Parsifal. Every record was
   read and marked by the author as *Accepted*, *Rejected* (with the exclusion criterion) or
   *Duplicated*. The Parsifal export with these decisions is `data/articles.xls`:
   1,697 records, 270 duplicates, 968 rejected, 459 accepted.
2. **Data extraction and quality assessment: Python scripts written by the author**
   (`scripts/`). The full text of the 459 accepted studies was converted to plain text, each
   study was matched to its record, and a rule-based extractor (regular expressions over the
   text, reference lists excluded) filled the extraction form and answered the seven quality
   questions. Eight studies had no retrievable full text; 11 scored below the 3.0 cut-off;
   440 entered the synthesis. The result is `data/studies_extraction.csv`.
3. **Second check of the extraction: Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic).** The output of the scripts
   was reviewed with the assistance of the model to confirm that the extracted fields and quality
   scores were consistent with the studies. The model did not run searches, did not select
   studies and did not perform the extraction. The 41 studies flagged by the first quality
   question (spatial data with a spatial database extension) were additionally read in full by
   the author, which reduced them to the 11 genuinely geospatial studies on which the
   geospatial analysis of the chapter is based.

## Search strings as executed

Common form (ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, SpringerLink), over title, abstract
and keywords:

```
("text-to-SQL" OR "text2SQL" OR "NL2SQL" OR "natural language to SQL")
AND ("large language model" OR LLM OR "language model" OR "fine-tuning" OR transformer)
AND (geospatial OR spatial OR geographic OR GIS OR dataset OR benchmark OR portuguese OR multilingual OR "real-world")
```

ScienceDirect (limit of eight Boolean connectors, no wildcards):

```
("text-to-SQL" OR "text2SQL" OR "NL2SQL" OR "natural language to SQL")
AND ("language model" OR "fine-tuning" OR geospatial OR spatial)
```

## Repository layout

```
data/
  articles.xls                 Parsifal export: every record with its selection status
  bibtex_exports/              raw BibTeX exports of the five libraries (19 July 2026)
  studies_extraction.csv       extraction form and quality scores, one row per study (451)
  references_included_studies.bib  BibTeX of the studies cited in the chapter (generated by 08_generate_bib.py)
scripts/
  config.py                    paths (all relative to the repository root)
  01_parse_bibtex.py           parse the raw exports -> work/bib_entries.json
  02_build_master.py           join the Parsifal decisions with the BibTeX metadata -> work/accepted.json
  03_convert_pdfs.sh           pdftotext over papers/<source>/*.pdf -> work/txt/
  04_match_pdfs.py             match each accepted record to its text file (title in header, proceedings volumes, fuzzy)
  05_build_corpus.py           cut one text segment per study -> work/corpus.json
  06_extract.py                quality questions QA1-QA7 and extraction fields -> work/extraction.json
  07_export_csv.py             -> data/studies_extraction.csv
  08_generate_bib.py           -> data/references_included_studies.bib (entries were merged into the thesis bibliography)
  09_summary_stats.py          the counts and percentages reported in the chapter
papers/                        full texts (not distributed; see papers/README.md)
work/                          intermediate files (ignored by git)
```

## Columns of `data/studies_extraction.csv`

`citation_key, title, year, venue, source, doi, study_type, included, QA1..QA7, quality_score,
approaches, models, datasets, metrics, languages, dbms, domains`. Multi-valued fields are
separated by `; `. `QA1..QA7` take the values 1.0 (yes), 0.5 (partially) and 0.0 (no);
`quality_score` is their sum and `included` is `yes` when the score is at least 3.0.

Quality questions: QA1 spatial data with a spatial DBMS extension; QA2 objectives clearly
stated; QA3 datasets identified and publicly available; QA4 proposal adequately described;
QA5 experimental evaluation with metrics; QA6 compared against baselines or prior work;
QA7 limitations and threats to validity.

## Reproducing

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt          # xlrd; pdftotext (poppler) must be on the PATH
# put the full texts in papers/<source>/ (see papers/README.md)
python scripts/01_parse_bibtex.py
python scripts/02_build_master.py
scripts/03_convert_pdfs.sh
python scripts/04_match_pdfs.py
python scripts/05_build_corpus.py
python scripts/06_extract.py
python scripts/07_export_csv.py
python scripts/08_generate_bib.py
python scripts/09_summary_stats.py
```

Two steps are matters of judgment and a replication should expect to diverge on them: the
screening decision under the criterion "does not address translation from natural language
into SQL", and the manual verification that separates substantive treatment of spatial data
from incidental mention. The spreadsheet exists so that both can be checked against the
studies rather than taken on trust.
